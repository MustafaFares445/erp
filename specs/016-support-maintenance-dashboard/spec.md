# Feature Specification: Support and Maintenance Dashboard

**Feature Directory**: `016-support-maintenance-dashboard`

**Created**: 2026-08-10

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: Extract the Support and Maintenance module from the canonical documentation set — `Docs/PRD.md` (features "Tickets" and "Maintenance", FR-017, FR-023), `Docs/SDD.md` §Tickets and §Maintenance, `Docs/database/ERD.md` (Support tables `tickets`, `ticket_messages`, `ticket_attachments`, `ticket_assignments`, `ticket_payment_links`, `maintenance_records`, `maintenance_tasks`, plus the status catalog and seed-data plan), `Docs/database/DFD.md` §Ticket and Maintenance, `Docs/api/API_CONTRACT.md` §Dashboard/§Customer ticket endpoints, `Docs/diagrams/SEQUENCE_DIAGRAMS.md` §15, and `Docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` §13. The seven decisions (D1–D7) recorded in §Owner Decisions below are project-owner approved on 2026-08-10 and binding; this specification encodes them rather than reopening them. ADR 0004 (`Docs/adr/0004-filament-support-maintenance-dashboard.md`) authorises the dashboard scope this specification covers.

## Owner Decisions

These decisions were taken by the project owner on 2026-08-10 and are settled inputs, not open questions.

- **D1 — Dashboard-only surface.** The module is delivered as `/admin` Filament surfaces only, authorised by ADR 0004, following the ADR 0002 (CRM) and ADR 0003 (Employees) precedent. The documented `/api/customer/tickets` and `/api/dashboard/tickets` endpoints, the customer app, and any employee/technician-facing API stay out of scope pending their own specification and ADR. Tickets and maintenance requests are logged in the dashboard on the customer's behalf.
- **D2 — "Service Records" is the ERD's `maintenance_tasks`.** The three reserved sidebar items map literally onto the three ERD Support entities: Tickets → `tickets`, Maintenance Requests → `maintenance_records`, Service Records → `maintenance_tasks`. No fourth work-history entity is introduced.
- **D3 — Priority and SLA are in scope.** Ticket priority and response/resolution due tracking are added on top of the ERD baseline, which carries neither.
- **D4 — Paid tickets are modelled without Stripe.** The documented `pending_payment` → `live` flow is built, but settlement is an admin-recorded manual entry. No Stripe call, no journal entry, no tax recognition, and no revenue recognition happens in this feature.
- **D5 — Equipment and warranty link.** A maintenance request may be linked to an existing serialized inventory unit, and carries an explicit warranty status.
- **D6 — Spare-parts consumption is in scope.** A service record may consume stock, and every such consumption posts an inventory movement through the existing Inventory services.
- **D7 — English-only UI for this phase**, matching the spec 013 and spec 015 precedent.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-08-13

- Q: Should the ticket SLA response/resolution clock run continuously (24/7 calendar time), or only during defined business hours? → A: Continuous calendar time; only the `waiting_customer` pause (FR-055) suspends it — no business-hours or holiday calendar is introduced.
- Q: What default first-response / resolution SLA targets should be seeded for each priority? → A: Urgent 1h / 4h; High 4h / 24h; Normal 8h / 48h; Low 24h / 72h (response / resolution).
- Q: When a spare-parts consumption is reversed (FR-086), must the entire recorded quantity be reversed at once, or can a partial quantity be reversed? → A: Full reversal only — a reversal always compensates the entire recorded quantity; a correction to a smaller amount is a full reversal followed by a new, smaller consumption.

## Scope

This feature adds the Support and Maintenance module to the existing `/admin` Filament dashboard, covering:

- support-ticket intake, classification, numbering, and triage;
- ticket assignment history and the full documented ticket status lifecycle;
- the ticket conversation thread and its attachments;
- paid tickets — payment-link records and admin-recorded settlement that unblocks the ticket;
- ticket priority, SLA response/resolution targets, clock pausing, and breach visibility;
- maintenance requests raised from a ticket or standalone, linked to a customer, product variant, serial number, serialized inventory unit, and warranty status;
- service records (the assigned maintenance work items) with technician, due date, and status;
- spare-parts consumption on a service record, posting inventory movements through existing Inventory services;
- search, filtering, reporting, and audit review across all of the above;
- fixed dashboard roles and permissions for the module.

The feature does **not** add: `/api/customer/*`, `/api/dashboard/*`, or any other API surface; the customer mobile application; a technician mobile application; customer self-service ticket creation; Stripe integration or payment links generated by a payment provider; journal entries, tax recognition, revenue recognition, or any accounting posting for ticket payments; outbound customer email, SMS, or push notification delivery; a knowledge base, canned responses, or chat/telephony integration; automatic ticket routing or AI triage. Adding any of these later requires its own specification and either a separate ADR or an explicit amendment to ADR 0004.

## User Scenarios and Testing

### User Story 1 - Enforce Dashboard Roles and Permissions (Priority: P1)

An authorized dashboard user's access to every ticket, payment-settlement, maintenance, service-record, parts-consumption, SLA, report, and audit action is governed by one of four fixed roles, checked consistently everywhere an action can be triggered.

**Why this priority**: Every other story writes customer-visible support records and, through spare parts, real stock. Without correct and consistently enforced permission checks, no other capability in this module can be trusted.

**Independent Test**: Sign in as each of the four fixed roles and exercise the same action through a direct page visit, a record action, a bulk action, and a direct service call that bypasses the UI, confirming identical allow/deny behavior across all four paths.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a System Admin, **when** using the dashboard, **then** full management, role management, payment settlement, restoration, and every override are available.
2. **Given** a Support Manager, **when** using the dashboard, **then** ticket creation, triage, assignment, closure, maintenance-request management, service-record planning, SLA policy management, and parts-consumption approval are available, and role management and record restoration are denied.
3. **Given** a Support Agent, **when** using the dashboard, **then** working a ticket assigned to them — posting messages, changing status within the allowed transitions, executing their service records, and recording parts used — is available, while assigning tickets to other agents, closing a ticket they do not own, editing SLA policy, and settling payments are denied.
4. **Given** a Reviewer, **when** using the dashboard, **then** all tickets, maintenance requests, service records, reports, and audit entries are viewable, and no create, edit, message, assignment, settlement, closure, or consumption action is available.
5. **Given** any role, **when** a page is opened or an action is executed (including a bulk action or a direct call that bypasses a hidden button), **then** the same permission boundary is enforced at both checkpoints.
6. **Given** a user whose only role is Support Manager or Support Agent, **when** they attempt to reach any Inventory, CRM, or Employees dashboard surface, **then** access is denied — recording parts consumption grants no Inventory module access of any kind.

### User Story 2 - Log and Classify a Support Ticket (Priority: P1)

A Support Manager or Support Agent logs a customer's support request as a ticket with its type, priority, description, and supporting files, and the system issues a unique ticket number.

**Why this priority**: The ticket is the root record of the entire module; assignment, conversation, payment, SLA, and every maintenance request either hang off a ticket or are measured against one.

**Independent Test**: Create, edit, search, filter, and cancel a ticket without assigning it, without payment, and without any maintenance record.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a new ticket, **when** it is saved, **then** it requires an existing customer, a ticket type, a priority, a title, and a description, and it is rejected with field-level messages when any is missing.
2. **Given** a saved ticket, **when** it is created, **then** the system assigns a unique, human-readable ticket number that is unique across active, cancelled, closed, and soft-deleted tickets.
3. **Given** two tickets created concurrently, **when** both are saved, **then** each receives a distinct ticket number and neither creation fails on a duplicate.
4. **Given** a ticket type of `maintenance_request`, **when** the ticket is saved, **then** it is flagged as maintenance-bound so a maintenance request can be raised from it without re-entering the customer or description.
5. **Given** a ticket, **when** files are attached, **then** each attachment records its file type and is retrievable, and non-permitted file types are rejected before any record is written.
6. **Given** the ticket list, **when** searched by ticket number, customer, or title, or filtered by status, type, priority, assignee, or SLA breach, **then** matching results are returned with pagination.
7. **Given** an existing ticket, **when** it is deleted, **then** it is archived rather than physically removed, its number stays reserved, and its conversation, assignments, and linked maintenance records remain intact.

### User Story 3 - Assign and Work a Ticket Through Its Lifecycle (Priority: P1)

A Support Manager assigns a ticket to an employee, and the assigned Support Agent moves it through the documented status lifecycle until it is resolved and closed, with every assignment and transition recorded.

**Why this priority**: The lifecycle is the module's core operational value and the source of every SLA measurement and support report; without it a ticket is only an inbox entry.

**Independent Test**: Take a ticket from `pending` to `closed` through assignment, work, a customer wait, resolution, and closure, plus attempt every disallowed transition, without touching payment, maintenance, or parts.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a ticket in `pending`, **when** it is triaged, **then** it moves to `live` and becomes eligible for assignment.
2. **Given** a ticket in `live`, **when** a Support Manager assigns it to an employee, **then** the ticket moves to `assigned`, its current assignee is set, and an append-only assignment record captures the assignee, the assigning user, and the assignment time.
3. **Given** an assigned ticket, **when** it is reassigned to a different employee, **then** a new assignment record is appended, the prior one is retained, and the ticket's current assignee reflects only the newest assignment.
4. **Given** an assigned ticket, **when** its assignee starts work, **then** it moves to `in_progress`.
5. **Given** a ticket in `in_progress`, **when** the agent needs customer input, **then** it moves to `waiting_customer`, and it returns to `in_progress` when the customer responds.
6. **Given** a ticket in `in_progress` or `waiting_customer`, **when** the agent completes the work, **then** it moves to `resolved` and its resolution timestamp is recorded.
7. **Given** a ticket in `resolved`, **when** the customer reports the problem persists, **then** it may be reopened to `in_progress` and its resolution timestamp is cleared.
8. **Given** a ticket in `closed`, **when** any reopening is attempted, **then** it is rejected; continuing the matter requires a new ticket that records its link to the closed one.
9. **Given** any ticket, **when** a transition outside the allowed set is attempted — including by a direct service call that bypasses the UI — **then** it is rejected with a message naming the current and attempted status, and no field is changed.
10. **Given** a ticket with an open linked maintenance request, **when** closure is attempted, **then** it is rejected until that maintenance request is closed or cancelled.

### User Story 4 - Hold a Paid Ticket Until Payment Is Recorded (Priority: P1)

A Support Manager marks a ticket as chargeable; the ticket is held at `pending_payment` with its reason and a payment-link record, and it becomes workable only after an authorized user records the settlement.

**Why this priority**: `Docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` §13 makes the unpaid-ticket hold an explicit acceptance criterion, and a chargeable ticket entering the work queue unpaid is a direct revenue leak.

**Independent Test**: Create a chargeable ticket, confirm it cannot be assigned or worked, record settlement, confirm it becomes workable, and attempt a duplicate settlement — all without any maintenance record or parts.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a new ticket marked chargeable with an amount and currency, **when** it is saved, **then** its status is `pending_payment`, its pending reason states that payment is awaited, and a payment-link record is created with that amount, currency, and a pending status.
2. **Given** a ticket in `pending_payment`, **when** assignment or any work transition is attempted, **then** it is rejected — a chargeable ticket cannot enter the work queue before settlement.
3. **Given** a ticket in `pending_payment`, **when** an authorized user records the settlement with its method reference, **then** the payment-link record moves to settled with the settling user and timestamp, the ticket moves to `live`, and its pending reason is cleared.
4. **Given** an already-settled payment link, **when** settlement is recorded again, **then** it is rejected and the ticket's status is unchanged — settlement is applied at most once.
5. **Given** a settlement, **when** it succeeds, **then** the payment-link update and the ticket transition are written in one transaction; if either fails, neither is left applied.
6. **Given** a ticket in `pending_payment` that the customer abandons, **when** it is cancelled, **then** the ticket moves to `cancelled` and its pending payment link is cancelled with it.
7. **Given** any recorded settlement, **when** it is written, **then** no journal entry, tax-recognition entry, revenue posting, or accounting side effect is produced by this feature.

### User Story 5 - Track Response and Resolution Against SLA (Priority: P2)

A Support Manager sets SLA targets per priority; every workable ticket carries a response and resolution due time, and breaches are visible in lists and reports.

**Why this priority**: SLA turns the lifecycle from a record into a commitment that can be managed, but tickets can be worked correctly without it, so it ranks below the lifecycle itself.

**Independent Test**: Configure targets for each priority, take one ticket to breach and one to compliance, pause one in `waiting_customer`, and raise one ticket's priority, verifying due times and flags at each step.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** SLA targets defined per priority, **when** a ticket becomes workable (`live`), **then** its response-due and resolution-due timestamps are computed from that moment and the targets in force are snapshotted onto the ticket.
2. **Given** a chargeable ticket, **when** it is held at `pending_payment`, **then** no SLA clock runs; the clock starts only when it reaches `live`.
3. **Given** a ticket with a response due time, **when** the first agent message is posted, **then** the first-response timestamp is recorded once and never overwritten by later messages.
4. **Given** a ticket that passes a due time without the corresponding event, **when** it is listed or reported, **then** it is flagged as response-breached or resolution-breached accordingly.
5. **Given** a ticket in `waiting_customer`, **when** time passes, **then** the resolution clock is paused, and on return to `in_progress` the remaining time is preserved rather than consumed by the wait.
6. **Given** a ticket whose priority is changed, **when** the change is saved, **then** the due times are recomputed from the original clock start using the new priority's targets and the change is audited; if the recomputation shows the ticket is already past due, it is flagged breached immediately rather than silently granted new time.
7. **Given** a ticket whose SLA has already been computed, **when** the SLA policy for that priority is later edited, **then** the ticket keeps its snapshotted targets and due times, so a policy change never rewrites history.

### User Story 6 - Raise and Track a Maintenance Request (Priority: P2)

A Support Manager raises a maintenance request for a customer's equipment — from an existing ticket or standalone — recording the product variant, serial number, serialized unit, warranty status, and issue description.

**Why this priority**: Maintenance is the second half of the module's documented purpose and the parent of every service record, but tickets deliver standalone value first.

**Independent Test**: Raise a maintenance request from a ticket and another standalone, link equipment and warranty, move it through its statuses, and cancel one — without creating any service record or consuming parts.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a ticket, **when** a maintenance request is raised from it, **then** the request is linked to that ticket and pre-fills the customer and description, and the link is visible from both records.
2. **Given** no ticket, **when** a standalone maintenance request is created, **then** it requires a customer and an issue description and is saved with no source ticket.
3. **Given** a maintenance request, **when** a serial number matching an existing serialized inventory unit is entered, **then** the request is linked to that unit and shows its product variant; when no unit matches, the serial number is retained as free text and the request is flagged as unlinked equipment rather than rejected.
4. **Given** a maintenance request, **when** it is saved, **then** it carries a warranty status of covered, expired, or unknown, defaulting to unknown, with covered requiring a warranty expiry date.
5. **Given** a maintenance request, **when** its status changes, **then** only `open → in_progress`, `open → cancelled`, `in_progress → closed`, and `in_progress → cancelled` are permitted; `closed` and `cancelled` are terminal.
6. **Given** a maintenance request with any service record that is not closed or cancelled, **when** closure is attempted, **then** it is rejected until every service record reaches a terminal status.
7. **Given** a maintenance request, **when** it is deleted, **then** it is archived rather than physically removed, and its service records and their recorded parts remain intact.

### User Story 7 - Plan and Execute Service Records (Priority: P2)

A Support Manager plans the work for a maintenance request as service records assigned to employees with due dates, and the assigned employee executes and closes each one.

**Why this priority**: Service records are how maintenance work is actually distributed and tracked, but a maintenance request is a usable record before the work is broken down.

**Independent Test**: Create service records under a maintenance request, assign, execute, and close them, and attempt an unauthorized execution and a disallowed transition — without consuming parts.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a maintenance request, **when** a service record is added, **then** it requires a title and belongs to exactly one maintenance request, and it may carry a description, an assigned employee, and a due date.
2. **Given** a service record with a due date, **when** the date is set, **then** it is rejected if it falls before its maintenance request's creation date.
3. **Given** a service record, **when** its status changes, **then** only `open → in_progress`, `open → cancelled`, `in_progress → closed`, and `in_progress → cancelled` are permitted, matching its maintenance request's status vocabulary.
4. **Given** a Support Agent, **when** they attempt to execute a service record assigned to a different employee, **then** the action is denied while their own service records remain workable.
5. **Given** service record views, **when** opened, **then** overdue, due-soon, and closed records are visually distinguished.
6. **Given** a maintenance request in `open` with a service record moved to `in_progress`, **when** the transition succeeds, **then** the parent maintenance request moves to `in_progress` in the same transaction.
7. **Given** a service record, **when** any status change occurs, **then** the actor, timestamp, and optional note are recorded in the audit trail.

### User Story 8 - Consume Spare Parts on a Service Record (Priority: P2)

An authorized user records the spare parts a service record consumed, decrementing stock from a named warehouse and creating the corresponding inventory movement.

**Why this priority**: Parts consumption closes the loop between support work and stock accuracy, but it is the one place where this module writes to Inventory, so it is deliberately layered on top of a working service-record flow.

**Independent Test**: Record a consumption against a service record, verify stock and the movement, attempt a consumption exceeding available stock, and reverse a consumption — using service records created by the prior story.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a service record, **when** a part consumption is recorded with a product variant, a warehouse, and a positive quantity, **then** stock for that variant and warehouse decreases by that quantity and exactly one inventory movement is created referencing the service record as its source.
2. **Given** a consumption whose quantity exceeds available stock in the chosen warehouse, **when** it is submitted, **then** it is rejected with a message naming the available quantity, and no stock change and no movement are written.
3. **Given** a consumption, **when** it succeeds, **then** the consumption record, the stock decrement, and the inventory movement are written inside one transaction; if any step fails, none of them persists.
4. **Given** a recorded consumption, **when** it is reversed, **then** a compensating inventory movement returns the stock, and neither the original consumption record nor its movement is deleted or edited.
5. **Given** a service record in `closed` or `cancelled`, **when** a new consumption is attempted, **then** it is rejected — parts are consumed only against work that is still open.
6. **Given** a user holding only the module's parts-consumption permission, **when** the consumption is recorded, **then** it succeeds without granting them any Inventory dashboard access.
7. **Given** a maintenance request or service record, **when** viewed, **then** every part consumed against it is listed with its variant, warehouse, quantity, actor, and timestamp.

---

### User Story 9 - Search, Report, and Audit Across the Module (Priority: P3)

Any authorized dashboard user searches and filters tickets, maintenance requests, and service records, and views open-workload, SLA-breach, resolution-time, and parts-consumption summaries.

**Why this priority**: Reporting makes the recorded data actionable day to day, but every record it reads is produced by the earlier stories.

**Independent Test**: Search and filter each of the three record types and open each report view using data created by the earlier stories.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the ticket, maintenance-request, and service-record lists, **when** searched or filtered, **then** matching results are returned with pagination.
2. **Given** the reporting views, **when** opened, **then** open ticket workload by status, by priority, and by assignee is shown.
3. **Given** the reporting views, **when** opened, **then** SLA response and resolution breach counts and average resolution time are shown for a chosen period.
4. **Given** the reporting views, **when** opened, **then** open maintenance requests, overdue service records, and parts consumed per period are shown.
5. **Given** any sensitive action in this module, **when** an authorized user opens the audit view for a record, **then** that action is retrievable with its actor, timestamp, and changed values.

## Edge Cases

- A ticket created as chargeable and later found to be covered under warranty is cancelled and re-logged as a non-chargeable ticket linked to the cancelled one; a `pending_payment` ticket is never silently converted to free, because the settlement record must stay truthful about what was and was not charged.
- A settlement recorded against a ticket that was cancelled between page load and submit is rejected, leaving the cancellation intact.
- Two users settling the same payment link concurrently result in exactly one settlement; the second is rejected as already settled rather than producing a second settled record or a second transition.
- A ticket reopened from `resolved` clears its resolution timestamp and resumes its original resolution clock; the reopening does not grant a fresh resolution window, and if the original due time has already passed the ticket is immediately flagged resolution-breached again.
- A ticket that sits in `waiting_customer` across an SLA policy edit keeps its snapshotted targets, so its remaining time on return to `in_progress` is computed from the values in force when its clock started.
- A ticket whose priority is lowered after breaching stays flagged as breached for the period it was breached; a priority change never erases a recorded breach.
- A maintenance request whose serialized unit is later disposed or adjusted out of inventory keeps its equipment link and its recorded serial number, because the service history must survive the unit's stock lifecycle.
- A serial number entered on a maintenance request that matches no serialized inventory unit is retained as free text with the request flagged as unlinked equipment, so a customer's untracked equipment is never a reason to refuse service.
- A warranty status of covered without an expiry date is rejected at save, so "covered" is never an unverifiable claim.
- Deleting a maintenance request that has recorded parts consumption archives it and leaves every inventory movement in place; stock history is never rewritten by a support-side deletion.
- Reversing a parts consumption after its service record is closed is permitted for a System Admin only, and creates a compensating movement rather than editing the original.
- A concurrent parts consumption that would drive available stock negative is rejected at write time, not only at form validation, so two agents consuming the last unit cannot both succeed.
- An unauthorized direct service call that bypasses a hidden or disabled UI control is rejected exactly as the equivalent UI action would be.
- Restoring an archived ticket or maintenance request returns it to its archived-from status, never directly to an active working status.

## Functional Requirements

### Roles and Authorization

- **FR-001**: The System Admin role MUST have full management access, role management, payment settlement, consumption reversal, and restoration capability across the module.
- **FR-002**: The Support Manager role MUST be able to create, triage, assign, and close tickets, manage maintenance requests and service records, manage SLA policy, and approve parts consumption.
- **FR-003**: The Support Agent role MUST be able to work tickets and service records assigned to them — posting messages, transitioning status within the allowed set, and recording parts consumption — and MUST NOT be able to assign work to other employees, settle payments, or edit SLA policy.
- **FR-004**: The Reviewer role MUST be able to view every record, report, and audit entry in the module with no create, edit, message, assignment, settlement, transition, or consumption capability.
- **FR-005**: The system MUST check permission both when a page is opened and when each action is executed.
- **FR-006**: Hiding a control MUST NOT be relied upon as the only means of preventing an action.
- **FR-007**: Bulk actions MUST be subject to the same permission checks as the equivalent individual action.
- **FR-008**: Every status transition in this module MUST be validated inside a domain service and a disallowed transition rejected with a message naming the current and attempted status, even when invoked directly rather than through the UI.
- **FR-009**: Adding the Support Manager or Support Agent role MUST NOT widen a user's access to the Inventory, CRM, or Employees modules; this cross-module boundary is a binding guarantee, not an implementation detail.

### Ticket Intake and Classification

- **FR-010**: The system MUST support creating a ticket with a customer, ticket type, priority, title, and description, all required.
- **FR-011**: The system MUST support the documented ticket types `software_issue`, `hardware_issue`, `general_support`, and `maintenance_request`, seeded as the default set.
- **FR-012**: The system MUST assign every ticket a unique, human-readable ticket number, allocated inside the creating transaction, unique across active, closed, cancelled, and archived tickets.
- **FR-013**: The system MUST support attaching files to a ticket, recording each attachment's file type, and MUST reject non-permitted file types before writing any record.
- **FR-014**: The system MUST support editing a ticket's classification data while it is not in a terminal status.
- **FR-015**: Deleting a ticket MUST archive it rather than physically remove it, keeping its number reserved and its conversation, assignments, payment links, and linked maintenance requests intact.
- **FR-016**: The system MUST support searching tickets by number, customer, or title and filtering by status, type, priority, assignee, and SLA breach state.
- **FR-017**: A ticket MUST be able to record a link to another ticket it continues or supersedes, so a closed matter can be carried forward without reopening a terminal record.

### Ticket Lifecycle and Assignment

- **FR-020**: The system MUST implement the documented ticket statuses `pending`, `pending_payment`, `live`, `assigned`, `in_progress`, `waiting_customer`, `resolved`, `closed`, and `cancelled`.
- **FR-021**: A non-chargeable ticket MUST be created in `pending`; a chargeable ticket MUST be created in `pending_payment`.
- **FR-022**: The system MUST permit only these transitions: `pending → live | cancelled`; `pending_payment → live | cancelled`; `live → assigned | cancelled`; `assigned → in_progress | live | cancelled`; `in_progress → waiting_customer | resolved | assigned | cancelled`; `waiting_customer → in_progress | resolved | cancelled`; `resolved → closed | in_progress`. `closed` and `cancelled` MUST be terminal.
- **FR-023**: Assigning a ticket MUST append an assignment record capturing the assigned employee, the assigning user, and the assignment time, and MUST NOT overwrite or delete any prior assignment record.
- **FR-024**: A ticket's current assignee MUST always reflect its newest assignment record.
- **FR-025**: The system MUST record a ticket's resolution timestamp when it enters `resolved` and clear it when the ticket is reopened to `in_progress`.
- **FR-026**: The system MUST reject closing a ticket that has a linked maintenance request not yet in a terminal status.
- **FR-027**: A ticket in `pending_payment` MUST NOT be assignable or transitionable into any working status until its payment is settled.
- **FR-028**: Every ticket status change MUST be written to the central audit trail with its actor, timestamp, and old and new values.

### Ticket Conversation and Attachments

- **FR-030**: The system MUST store a ticket's messages as an append-only thread, each recording its sender and creation time.
- **FR-031**: The system MUST distinguish an internal note from a customer-visible message, so agents can record context that is not part of the customer conversation.
- **FR-032**: A ticket message MUST NOT be editable or deletable once posted; a correction is posted as a new message.
- **FR-033**: The system MUST record a ticket's first agent message time once, as its first-response timestamp, and MUST NOT overwrite it with any later message.
- **FR-034**: An internal note MUST NOT satisfy the first-response requirement; only a customer-visible agent message MUST set the first-response timestamp.
- **FR-035**: The system MUST support attaching files to a ticket and displaying them alongside the conversation.

### Paid Tickets

- **FR-040**: The system MUST support marking a ticket chargeable with an amount and a currency at creation.
- **FR-041**: A chargeable ticket MUST create a payment-link record carrying its amount, currency, and status, and MUST record the ticket's pending reason.
- **FR-042**: The system MUST support recording a manual settlement against a pending payment link, capturing the settling user, the settlement timestamp, and a payment method reference.
- **FR-043**: Recording a settlement MUST move the payment link to settled and the ticket from `pending_payment` to `live`, clearing the ticket's pending reason, inside a single transaction.
- **FR-044**: Settlement MUST be idempotent at the record level: a payment link that is already settled MUST reject any further settlement, leaving the ticket unchanged.
- **FR-045**: Cancelling a ticket in `pending_payment` MUST cancel its pending payment link in the same transaction.
- **FR-046**: This feature MUST NOT create a journal entry, a tax-recognition entry, a revenue posting, or any other accounting side effect from a recorded settlement; a recorded settlement is an operational unblock only.
- **FR-047**: The payment-link record MUST reserve, unused, the fields the future Stripe integration requires (external payment record reference and payment URL), so the Payments module can adopt the record without a schema migration of this module's own design.
- **FR-048**: Every settlement and every settlement rejection MUST be written to the central audit trail with its actor and timestamp.

### Priority and SLA

- **FR-050**: The system MUST support the ticket priorities Low, Normal, High, and Urgent.
- **FR-051**: The system MUST support an SLA policy defining a first-response target and a resolution target per priority, manageable by a Support Manager and seeded with these defaults: Urgent 1h response / 4h resolution; High 4h / 24h; Normal 8h / 48h; Low 24h / 72h.
- **FR-052**: The SLA clock MUST start when a ticket first reaches `live`, never at creation, so a chargeable ticket held at `pending_payment` consumes none of its target. The clock MUST run in continuous, 24/7 calendar time; the only pause is the `waiting_customer` suspension defined in FR-055, with no business-hours or holiday calendar applied.
- **FR-053**: The system MUST snapshot the SLA targets in force onto the ticket when its clock starts, and MUST NOT let a later policy edit change any already-started ticket's due times.
- **FR-054**: The system MUST compute and store a response-due and a resolution-due timestamp, and MUST flag a ticket response-breached or resolution-breached when the corresponding event has not occurred by its due time.
- **FR-055**: Time spent in `waiting_customer` MUST NOT consume the resolution target; the paused duration MUST be accumulated and the resolution-due timestamp extended by it on return to a working status.
- **FR-056**: Changing a ticket's priority MUST recompute its due times from the original clock start using the new priority's snapshotted-at-change targets, MUST audit the change, and MUST flag the ticket breached immediately when the recomputation shows it is already past due.
- **FR-057**: A recorded breach MUST NOT be erased by a later priority change, reopening, or policy edit.
- **FR-058**: Reopening a `resolved` ticket MUST resume its original resolution clock rather than granting a fresh resolution window.

### Maintenance Requests, Equipment, and Warranty

- **FR-060**: The system MUST support creating a maintenance request from a ticket, pre-filling the customer and description and recording the source-ticket link on both records.
- **FR-061**: The system MUST support creating a standalone maintenance request with a customer and an issue description and no source ticket.
- **FR-062**: The system MUST support recording a maintenance request's product variant and equipment serial number.
- **FR-063**: The system MUST link a maintenance request to an existing serialized inventory unit when its serial number matches one, and MUST retain an unmatched serial number as free text with the request flagged as unlinked equipment rather than rejecting it.
- **FR-064**: A maintenance request MUST carry a warranty status of covered, expired, or unknown, defaulting to unknown, and MUST require a warranty expiry date when the status is covered.
- **FR-065**: The system MUST implement the documented maintenance statuses `open`, `in_progress`, `closed`, and `cancelled`, permitting only `open → in_progress | cancelled` and `in_progress → closed | cancelled`, with `closed` and `cancelled` terminal.
- **FR-066**: The system MUST reject closing a maintenance request while any of its service records is not in a terminal status.
- **FR-067**: Deleting a maintenance request MUST archive it rather than physically remove it, retaining its service records, recorded parts, and every inventory movement they produced.
- **FR-068**: A maintenance request's equipment link MUST survive a change to the serialized unit's stock status, including disposal or adjustment out of inventory.

### Service Records

- **FR-070**: The system MUST support creating a service record inside a maintenance request with a required title and an optional description, assigned employee, and due date.
- **FR-071**: A service record MUST belong to exactly one maintenance request and MUST NOT be movable between maintenance requests.
- **FR-072**: A service record's due date MUST NOT fall before its maintenance request's creation date.
- **FR-073**: The system MUST implement service-record statuses matching the maintenance vocabulary — `open`, `in_progress`, `closed`, `cancelled` — permitting only `open → in_progress | cancelled` and `in_progress → closed | cancelled`.
- **FR-074**: Moving the first service record of an `open` maintenance request to `in_progress` MUST move the maintenance request to `in_progress` in the same transaction.
- **FR-075**: A Support Agent MUST be able to execute only service records assigned to them; executing another employee's service record MUST be denied.
- **FR-076**: Service-record views MUST distinguish overdue, due-soon, and closed records.
- **FR-077**: Every service-record status change MUST be written to the central audit trail with its actor, timestamp, and optional note.

### Spare-Parts Consumption

- **FR-080**: The system MUST support recording a part consumption against a service record with a product variant, a warehouse, and a positive quantity.
- **FR-081**: Every recorded consumption MUST create exactly one inventory movement through the existing Inventory services, referencing the service record as its source, so no stock change in this module bypasses the movement ledger.
- **FR-082**: A consumption exceeding available stock for the chosen variant and warehouse MUST be rejected with a message naming the available quantity, writing no stock change and no movement.
- **FR-083**: The consumption record, the stock decrement, and the inventory movement MUST be written inside a single transaction; a failure at any step MUST leave none of them persisted.
- **FR-084**: The availability check MUST be enforced at write time under concurrency, not only at form validation.
- **FR-085**: A consumption MUST NOT be recorded against a service record in a terminal status.
- **FR-086**: Reversing a consumption MUST create a compensating inventory movement and MUST NOT delete or edit the original consumption record or its movement; reversal after the service record is closed MUST require the System Admin role. A reversal MUST always compensate the entire recorded quantity of the original consumption; partial-quantity reversal is not supported — a correction to a smaller amount MUST be recorded as a full reversal followed by a new, smaller consumption.
- **FR-087**: The system MUST list every consumption recorded against a maintenance request and its service records, with variant, warehouse, quantity, actor, and timestamp.
- **FR-088**: Recording a consumption MUST require only this module's parts-consumption permission and MUST NOT grant or require any Inventory dashboard access.

### Search, Reports, and Audit

- **FR-090**: The system MUST support search and filtering across tickets, maintenance requests, and service records with pagination.
- **FR-091**: The system MUST report open ticket workload by status, by priority, and by assignee.
- **FR-092**: The system MUST report SLA response and resolution breach counts and average resolution time for a chosen period.
- **FR-093**: The system MUST report open maintenance requests, overdue service records, and parts consumed for a chosen period.
- **FR-094**: The system MUST expose a reviewable audit trail for every sensitive action in this module to authorized users.

### General

- **FR-100**: The system MUST present clear, field-level validation and error messages.
- **FR-101**: Every page and every action MUST be protected by permission checks.
- **FR-102**: Every operation that writes more than one record MUST run inside a database transaction, leaving no partial save.
- **FR-103**: Ticket attachments MUST be stored and retrievable through the existing media infrastructure; no feature-specific file table may be created.
- **FR-104**: Authorization for every action in this module MUST be enforced exclusively through a Spatie Permission-backed `support.*` permission catalogue, every sensitive action's audit trail MUST be written exclusively through the shared `activity()`-based audit trail (`spatie/laravel-activitylog`, per ADR 0005), and every attachment MUST be stored exclusively through Spatie Media Library. No feature-specific alternative mechanism for authorization, audit, or file storage is permitted.
- **FR-105**: Wherever this specification requires a recorded decision — payment settlement, consumption reversal, closure, or cancellation — that record MUST capture at minimum the deciding user and the decision timestamp.
- **FR-106**: All dashboard labels, validation messages, and reports for this module MUST be delivered in English for this phase.
- **FR-107**: Lists MUST support search, filtering, and pagination.

## Key Entities

- **Ticket**: A customer's support request — number, customer, type, priority, title, description, chargeability, pending reason, current assignee, status, SLA snapshot and due times, first-response and resolution timestamps, and optional link to a continued ticket.
- **Ticket Message**: An append-only entry in a ticket's conversation, with sender, body, creation time, and whether it is customer-visible or an internal note.
- **Ticket Attachment**: A file linked to a ticket, with its file type, stored through the shared media infrastructure.
- **Ticket Assignment**: An append-only record of a ticket being assigned to an employee, with the assigning user and assignment time.
- **Ticket Payment Link**: A chargeable ticket's payment record — amount, currency, status, settling user, settlement time, method reference — with the external payment reference and payment URL reserved for the future Payments module.
- **SLA Policy**: The first-response and resolution targets for one priority, seeded with a default and editable by a Support Manager; the values in force are snapshotted onto each ticket when its clock starts.
- **Maintenance Request**: A service need for a customer's equipment — optional source ticket, customer, product variant, serial number, optional serialized-unit link, warranty status and expiry, issue description, and status.
- **Service Record**: An assigned unit of maintenance work inside a maintenance request — title, description, assigned employee, due date, and status. This is the ERD's `maintenance_tasks` entity.
- **Service Record Part**: A quantity of a product variant consumed from a warehouse against a service record, paired with the inventory movement it produced and any compensating reversal movement.
- **Support Report**: A read-only aggregate view (open workload, SLA breaches, average resolution time, overdue service records, parts consumed) with no backing table of its own.

## Assumptions and Dependencies

- The existing `/admin` Filament panel, Spatie Permission, Spatie Media Library, `AuditLogger`, and `TracksBlameable` infrastructure remain canonical and are extended, not duplicated, by this feature.
- `CustomerProfile`, `EmployeeProfile`, `ProductVariant`, `Warehouse`, and `SerializedInventoryUnit` already exist and are reused; this feature creates none of them and changes none of their existing behavior.
- Parts consumption is posted through the existing Inventory movement services, which own the stock source of truth (`product_variant_id + warehouse_id`) per Constitution Principle III. This module never writes stock rows directly.
- No Sales, Invoice, Payment, or Stripe module exists in the codebase yet. Ticket settlement is therefore an operational record only, and warranty coverage cannot be derived from a sales document. Warranty status is entered explicitly on the maintenance request; automatic derivation from the sale arrives with the Sales/Invoice module and is out of scope here.
- The `serialized_inventory_units` table carries no warranty, sale, or customer-ownership data today. Linking a maintenance request to a unit therefore identifies the equipment; it does not by itself establish who owns it or whether it is in warranty.
- No notification infrastructure exists yet. SLA breaches, assignments, and closures are surfaced in the dashboard only; outbound email, SMS, and push delivery arrive with the Reporting and Notifications feature.
- Customers do not access this module. Every ticket, message, and maintenance request is entered by a dashboard user on the customer's behalf until the customer app is separately specified and approved.
- Ticket types are seeded as the four documented defaults and are treated as a fixed catalogue for this phase.
- The ERD is extended by this feature in four documented places: ticket priority and SLA fields, the SLA policy table, the maintenance-request warranty and serialized-unit fields, and the service-record parts table. Every other structure follows `Docs/database/ERD.md` as written. These extensions are recorded in ADR 0004 and must be reflected back into the ERD when this feature is planned.
- The seven decisions (D1–D7) recorded above are approved and are settled inputs to this specification, not open questions.

## Success Criteria

- **SC-001**: A Support Manager can log a fully classified ticket with a guaranteed-unique number in under two minutes, with no two tickets ever able to share a number, including against cancelled, closed, and archived tickets, and including under concurrent creation.
- **SC-002**: A chargeable ticket is never workable before settlement: across 100% of tested attempts, assignment and every working transition are rejected while the ticket is in `pending_payment`.
- **SC-003**: Settlement is applied at most once per payment link, verified under concurrent submission, with the ticket transition and the link update either both applied or both absent.
- **SC-004**: No recorded settlement produces any journal entry, tax-recognition entry, or accounting side effect, verified by a dedicated regression test.
- **SC-005**: Every disallowed status transition for tickets, maintenance requests, and service records is rejected at the domain-service layer, verified for 100% of the transition pairs outside the permitted sets in FR-022, FR-065, and FR-073, including when invoked directly rather than through the UI.
- **SC-006**: A ticket's SLA due times never change as a result of a later SLA policy edit, verified for tickets in every non-terminal status.
- **SC-007**: Time spent in `waiting_customer` never consumes the resolution target, and a reopened ticket never receives a fresh resolution window.
- **SC-008**: Every recorded parts consumption produces exactly one inventory movement, and no consumption ever leaves stock changed without a movement or a movement without a stock change — verified including the rejection, failure, and reversal paths.
- **SC-009**: A consumption that would drive available stock negative is rejected under concurrent submission, with no partial write, in 100% of tested attempts.
- **SC-010**: Every one of the four fixed roles is denied on 100% of actions outside its matrix and permitted on 100% of actions inside it, verified at both page-open and action-execution checkpoints.
- **SC-011**: A dashboard user whose only role is Support Manager or Support Agent gains zero additional Inventory, CRM, or Employees module access, verified by a dedicated regression test for each role.
- **SC-012**: Every sensitive action — ticket creation, transition, assignment, settlement, closure, maintenance transition, service-record transition, parts consumption, and consumption reversal — produces a retrievable audit entry with actor, timestamp, and changed values.
- **SC-013**: No ticket, maintenance request, or service record is ever physically deleted; every deletion is retrievable as an archived record with its history intact.
- **SC-014**: The `composer test` quality gate completes successfully without lowering the project's existing 100% type-coverage or code-coverage thresholds and without growing the PHPStan baseline.
